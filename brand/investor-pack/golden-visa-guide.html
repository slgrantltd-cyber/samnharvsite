What the Golden Visa gives you

  • Ten years of residency, renewable — not tied to a job or employer.
  • Family included — spouse and children sponsored under your visa.
  • No minimum stay — live in Dubai full time, or just keep it as your base.
  • Emirates ID, bank accounts, business set-up — you operate as a resident.

The rule: AED 2 million of property

Roughly £420,000, measured on the registered price on your title deed — not today’s market value. The parts people don’t know:

  • You can combine properties — two flats at 1M each qualifies.
  • Off-plan counts — even with instalments still running.
  • Mortgaged counts — it’s the property’s value that matters, not your equity.
  • Freehold areas only — most areas sold to international buyers qualify; always confirm first.

New in 2026 — most guides haven’t caught up

In February 2026 the old rule that you must have already paid 50% of the price was scrapped. Qualification is now simply the property’s registered value reaching AED 2M — mortgaged or mid-payment included. The visa got easier this year.

The process — six steps

1Own or buy qualifying property — AED 2M+ registered, freehold area.
2Title deed in order (or Oqood for off-plan) — the application rests on it.
3Land Department approval letter — confirms your property qualifies.
4Visa application — passport, photos, health insurance, the approval letter.
5Medical test & fingerprints — a morning at an approved centre.
6Visa & Emirates ID issued — family applications can follow immediately.

Time: about 2–4 weeks end to end. Cost: roughly AED 10,000 in government fees for the main applicant, plus the medical (≈AED 700), insurance, and a few thousand per family member.

Renewing — and switching property

The part almost nobody explains properly:

  • Renewal is straightforward if you still qualify. Still holding AED 2M+ of registered property when the ten years are up? You renew for another ten. Same rule, checked again.
  • Selling without a plan is how people lose it. Drop below the threshold and the visa won’t survive — and everything attached to it (family sponsorship, ID, banking) unwinds with it.
  • The fix is sequencing. Selling to upgrade? Structure it so the new qualifying purchase registers as the old one goes — your residency never blinks. The order of the two transfers is everything, and it’s planned before listing, not after selling.
  • Buying another property anyway? If the new purchase clears AED 2M on its own, your visa stops depending on the old property at all — which quietly frees you to sell it whenever the market suits, not whenever the visa allows.

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming market value counts. It’s the registered purchase price on the deed. “It’s worth 2.1M now” doesn’t qualify a 1.9M purchase.
  • Buying the visa, not the property. Any flat over 2M gets the visa — only a good one is also a sound £400k investment. Check the building’s service charge (Dubai publishes every building’s in an official index), the developer’s track record, and the realistic rent first.
  • Selling before sequencing. If the visa matters to you, the sale and the next purchase are one plan, not two events.
