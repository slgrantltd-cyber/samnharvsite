How buying off-plan actually works

Buying off-plan in Dubai is a regulated, escrow-protected process — but it moves fast at launch, and knowing the sequence in advance is the difference between choosing a unit and being sold one.

1
Brief & budget. We agree what the money needs to do — growth or income — your ceiling, and your exit horizon. This happens before any brochure is opened.
2
Expression of Interest (EOI). A refundable deposit (typically AED 20,000–100,000 depending on the project) registers you for a launch and sets your place in the queue for unit selection.
3
Launch day & allocation. Units are released against the EOI queue. You confirm from the real price sheet — unit, floor, view, exact price — not from the marketing render.
4
Booking & down payment. You sign the booking form and pay the down payment (usually 10–20%), plus the 4% Dubai Land Department fee and admin charges.
5
SPA — the Sale & Purchase Agreement. The full contract: specification, floor plan, payment schedule, completion date and compensation terms. This is the document worth reading twice; we go through it with you.
6
Oqood registration. Your purchase is registered with the DLD in the interim register — your legal record of ownership while the building goes up.
7
Staged payments — into escrow. Instalments follow the plan in the SPA (e.g. 1% a month, or construction milestones). By law they go into a RERA-supervised escrow account the developer can only draw against verified construction progress.
8
Handover. Snagging inspection, final payment, title deed issued, keys. If it’s a rental, furnishing and lettings start here — we can arrange both through our partners.

Residency through property
The visa, in plain terms
10-year Golden Visa

Own property worth AED 2 million+ (≈£420k) recorded at the DLD. Can be one property or several combined; off-plan and mortgaged property can qualify (with proof of AED 2m paid, for mortgages). Freehold areas only. Renewable, family sponsorship included, no minimum stay beyond periodic visits.

2-year investor residency

The traditional route for smaller holdings (historically AED 750k+). From April 2026 Dubai relaxed the minimum for fully-owned property — making residency accessible at lower entry points. Requirements are checked case-by-case at application.

Immigration rules change and are applied case-by-case — we confirm the current position with the developer and a registered agent before you commit, never after.

Before you fly
Visiting Dubai to view

When. October to April is the season — 25–32°C. June to September is 40°C+; viewings still run, everything is air-conditioned, flights and hotels are cheaper.

Getting in. UK passports get a free 30–40 day visa on arrival. The weekend is Saturday–Sunday; government offices close then.

Getting around. Careem/Uber everywhere and cheap; the Metro covers the Marina–Downtown spine. Allow 45 minutes between viewings — Dubai distances deceive.

Viewing days. Bring your passport — sales centres register visitors. Two or three projects a day maximum if you want to actually think. Sales suites are theatre; ask for the price sheet and the service-charge figure.

Money. Cards everywhere. If you commit while out there, deposits are paid by card or transfer — never cash, and never to a personal account.

Manner. Dubai is easy-going but it is not Ibiza — dress smart-casual at sales centres and hotels and you’ll be treated accordingly. During Ramadan, eat and drink discreetly in public by day.
