## DIFC

### D1 — "The district that sells out in 48 hours" (scarcity)
**VO:**
There's a square mile of Dubai where every apartment launch for the last three years has sold out almost instantly — and almost nobody outside finance knows why.
It's DIFC — the financial centre. For twenty years it built offices, not homes. Today over 50,000 people work inside it… and there are only around eight residential towers for all of them.
So when DIFC finally launched its first-ever apartments in 2023, from one and a half million dirhams — gone within days.
2025, Heights Tower, 366 apartments on the last plot of the original district — sold out in about 48 hours.
February this year, The Residences in the new Zabeel District — 463 homes, three thousand brokers at the preview — sold out at launch.
That's not marketing. That's fifty thousand salaries chasing eight buildings.
And the next part is the biggest of all — that's the next video.

**Animation notes:** open on a glowing square-mile outline on a dark Dubai map; counter ticking 50,200 workers vs 8 towers; three launch cards stamping SOLD OUT in sequence with dates; end on a "next chapter" tease frame.

### D2 — "Dubai is spending 100 billion on one district" (Zabeel District)
**VO:**
In January, Dubai's ruler approved a project worth over 100 billion dirhams — and it's not a new island. It's an extension of the financial district.
It's called Zabeel District. Seven point one million square feet of land, built in six phases, designed to hold another 42,000 companies and 125,000 workers — including what DIFC calls the world's first purpose-built AI campus.
Opens to the public in 2030. The full masterplan runs to 2040.
Now think about what that does to homes next to it. DIFC rents already run around 122 to 145 thousand a year for a one-bed, according to Bayut. Brokers quoted in Khaleej Times expect the new district's apartments at around three thousand seven hundred per square foot — and the first 463 sold out in February anyway.
A hundred billion builds the offices. It doesn't build enough bedrooms. Save this one for 2030.

**Animation notes:** number 100,000,000,000 assembling from particles; masterplan footprint growing in six phase-blocks with a 2030/2040 timeline bar; split-screen offices vs bedrooms imbalance scales; end card "2030" with save icon pulse.

### D3 — "The part of Dubai that runs on English law" (common law)
**VO:**
There's one part of Dubai where UAE law doesn't apply to your property — and international buyers pay a premium for exactly that.
Inside DIFC, everything runs on English common law. It has its own courts — in English, with its own judges — operating since 2006. Your title deed isn't even registered with the Dubai Land Department; DIFC has its own property registrar.
Why does that matter? Because a banker from London or a fund manager from Singapore already understands every word of their contract. No translation, no unfamiliar system — the same legal framework as home.
The trade-off: transfers inside DIFC cost 5 percent instead of Dubai's usual 4.
One extra percent, for the only neighbourhood in the city where a hundred and two hedge funds feel legally at home.
That's why DIFC homes don't stay on the market.

**Animation notes:** Dubai map with DIFC cut out and lifted like a separate layer; gavel/scales motif morphing into a title deed; "5% vs 4%" comparison plate; close on the lifted layer clicking back in.

## Lunaya by Zaya

*Context locked in: Lunaya = lagoon community in Saih Shuaib 1, Jebel Ali, directly on Sheikh Zayed Road, ~7 min from Palm Jebel Ali (developer figure). JV: ZAYA (Nadia Zaal) x FIVE Holdings. ~550 villas/townhouses in four types + Lunaya Terraces apartments (from ~AED 2.07M, 25/75 plan). Villas launched 4.5M, now from ~4.9M, 40/60 plan. Handover from 2029. 900,000 sqft lagoon, 65% green space, 4.5 km trail. NEVER: name an architect (none published), present Nurai resort as currently open (closed for renovation since 2024, owned by Aldar since 2022), quote a price per sqft, or state the "AED 4bn in 48 hours" sales claim without "the developer says".*

### L1 — "The woman who built a private island" (the Zaya story)
**VO:**
The woman who built a private island off Abu Dhabi has just launched her biggest project ever — on the other side of Dubai to everyone else.
Nadia Zaal. She created Zaya Nurai Island — a resort island you could only reach by boat — and sold it to Aldar in 2022 for a reported 600 million dirhams. She runs Al Barari, which markets itself as the greenest community in Dubai. Forbes ranks her among the most powerful businesswomen in the Middle East.
Her new one is called Lunaya — around 550 villas and townhouses on Sheikh Zayed Road in Jebel Ali, seven minutes from Palm Jebel Ali, built with FIVE — the hotel group.
The centrepiece: a 900,000 square foot swimmable lagoon, with 65 percent of the land kept green.
Everyone builds towers. She builds places people don't want to leave. Watch what happens to this corner of the city.

**Animation notes:** island silhouette rising from water → morph into portrait frame → timeline of Nurai/Al Barari/Hameni logos; map pin sliding down SZR to Jebel Ali; lagoon filling in masterplan top-view; end on slow zoom-out of the green masterplan.

### L2 — "Why the smart money moved to the Jebel Ali end" (the location bet)
**VO:**
Everyone's watching Downtown. The developers are quietly buying the other end of Sheikh Zayed Road — and one project tells you why.
Lunaya, by ZAYA and FIVE, sits right on Sheikh Zayed Road in Jebel Ali — about seven minutes from Palm Jebel Ali, where the next decade of Dubai's waterfront is being built, and under 25 minutes from Al Maktoum airport, which Dubai is turning into the biggest airport in the world.
Villas there launched at four and a half million at the start of 2026. Months later they're marketed from about four point nine. The apartments by the lagoon start around two point one million — which also clears the two-million Golden Visa line.
Handover runs from 2029 — right as that end of the city switches on.
The last time Dubai grew in one direction, the people who bought early on the road there did very well. Would you buy the growth end or the finished end? Tell me below.

**Animation notes:** arrow sweeping along a stylised SZR from Downtown to Jebel Ali; pulse markers on Palm Jebel Ali + airport; price plate animating 4.5M → ~4.9M; calendar flip to 2029; comment-prompt end card.

### L3 — "A 900,000 square foot swimming pool" (the lagoon lifestyle)
**VO:**
One new Dubai community is building a lagoon the size of twelve football pitches — and it's swimmable.
It's called Lunaya, in Jebel Ali — by ZAYA, the developer behind Al Barari's team, together with FIVE. Nine hundred thousand square feet of crystal lagoon in the middle, 65 percent of the whole site kept as green space, a four and a half kilometre trail around it.
Then the details: an outdoor cinema on the lagoon, padel and pickleball courts, a FIVE-branded spa, a dog park, a kids' adventure zone.
Townhouses from around four point nine million. Lagoon-side apartments from around two point one — on a plan where 75 percent isn't due until handover from 2029.
Here's the question nobody asks: in five years, which is rarer in Dubai — another tower… or a home on water this size?
Exactly.

**Animation notes:** aerial lagoon reveal with "12 football pitches" overlay comparison; amenity icons orbiting the lagoon; split price plates (villas / apartments / 25-75 plan); end freezes on the rhetorical question text, hard cut.
