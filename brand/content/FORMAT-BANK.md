# FORMAT BANK — evidence-backed video formats (researched 18 Sep 2026)
*Four research lanes, 47 web searches. Every format: proof it works → the mechanics → hook shapes → Samuel's version. Style rules apply on top: plain English, no wordplay, real sourced numbers, his voice.*


## PROPERTY

### Price-anchored comparison: "What AED X buys you in [area]"
**Evidence:** PropCRM's Dubai agent marketing guide calls "What AED X buys you in [Community]" the single most reliably viral Dubai real-estate format, with multi-price-point roundups pulling millions of views; Realting's international ranking of realtor creators confirms short price-comparison tours and deal breakdowns are the top-impression category on TikTok/IG. The mechanic was proven at scale by NYC broker Cash Jordan (15M+ views, covered by PIX11 and The Real Deal), whose price-reveal tours built his whole business.
**Mechanics:** Show 3-5 real listings at set price points in one area, with the price held back or escalating so the viewer watches to the end to see what the money actually buys. The price reveal creates a curiosity loop and sparks comparison comments from viewers in other cities, which drives shares far beyond the local market.
**Hook shapes:** This is what AED 1 million actually gets you in Dubai Marina. · Same budget, three different areas of Dubai. The difference shocked me. · £300k in Bristol vs AED 1.5m in JVC. Watch this.
**Samuel's version:** A weekly series comparing UK money to Dubai property, using his own relocation as the anchor. Opening line: "I just sold my car and my flat in the UK. Here's exactly what that money buys me in Dubai." Then three real listings with sourced AED prices, service charges, and yield on screen, fast cuts, ending with which one he'd actually buy and why.

### Blunt POV tour: honest walkthrough that calls out flaws
**Evidence:** Cash Jordan's "worst apartment ever" tours got national press (Fox News) precisely because he says it like he sees it; DC agent Daniel Heider built 3.7M TikTok followers and 61M views on listing videos (Axios). Enes Yilmazer (5.9M subs, ~15M views/month per CreatorDB, cinematic tours regularly past 10M views including Dubai properties like the $135M Bvlgari mansion) proves the tour format, and reaction channels making "Flaws Exposed" versions of his tours show honesty is the differentiator. In a Dubai market that a French buyer-beware piece (dubai-immo.com) says is flooded with too-polished Instagram property videos, blunt honesty is the gap.
**Mechanics:** Walk a property POV-style, phone-first, and narrate what's actually good and actually bad — the thin walls, the service charge, the view that's really a construction site. Trust comes from being the one broker willing to say a listing is overpriced.
**Hook shapes:** Every agent in Dubai will show you this apartment. Nobody shows you this bit. · This tower looks incredible online. Here's what the listing photos hide. · I'm a broker and I wouldn't buy this. Here's why.
**Samuel's version:** "Would I buy it?" tours once he's on the ground: 45-second POV walkthroughs of real Dubai listings ending with a straight yes/no and one reason. Opening line: "This apartment is listed at 1.4 million dirhams. I'm going to show you the three things the photos don't." His Michelin background gives a natural credibility line — he spent years in kitchens where detail was everything, so he checks the finish, not the lobby.

### Deal breakdown with real numbers on screen
**Evidence:** Realting's analysis names deal breakdowns alongside tours as the formats gaining millions of impressions for realtors; finance-shorts research (Fluxnote) shows number-led hooks with a surprising statistic in the first 3 seconds are what makes money content travel, and real-estate reel studies (Reel-E, Aumovo) find educational numbers content is the most-saved category, which drives algorithmic distribution. UK property TikTok has viral precedents like the "I bought a hotel for £1" purchase-option breakdown.
**Mechanics:** Take one real deal and put every number on screen fast: purchase price, costs, income, net. Savers and sharers treat it as free education; the specificity is the proof and the hook.
**Hook shapes:** This flat makes £2,100 a month. Here's every number. · I run a £250k-a-year property business. This is where the money actually goes. · This Dubai off-plan deal looks great until you see line four.
**Samuel's version:** He has a genuinely rare asset: a real £250k/yr, ~75% margin UK serviced-accommodation business with councils. Opening line: "My property company turns over £250,000 a year working with councils. Here's the actual breakdown." Desk-studio format, numbers stacking on screen, then the pivot: the same analysis applied to real Dubai listings — purchase price, fees, realistic rent, true net yield — so buyers learn to read a deal before they buy one.

### Market Minute: fast market update with sourced stats
**Evidence:** PropertyCine's YouTube Shorts strategy guide identifies "Market Minute" updates (local trends, rates, prices in under 60 seconds) as a core viral-and-lead format for agents, and Luxury Presence documents agents getting offers before listings hit MLS off the back of market videos; the housing-market Shorts ecosystem (dozens of high-frequency update channels) shows durable demand for someone who reads the data for you.
**Mechanics:** One stat, one chart or headline on screen, a plain explanation of what it means for buyers, delivered fast and on a schedule so viewers follow for the next one. Consistency turns it into appointment viewing and positions the creator as the expert.
**Hook shapes:** Dubai property prices did something this month nobody's talking about. · New DLD numbers just dropped. Here's the one that matters if you're buying. · Everyone says Dubai is a bubble. Here's what the transaction data actually shows.
**Samuel's version:** A twice-weekly 40-second desk-studio update using only sourced numbers (DLD transaction data, Property Monitor, rate changes), aimed squarely at buyers, not investors' hype. Opening line: "Dubai did 12,000 property transactions last month. Here's what that means if you're thinking of buying." One number, one plain takeaway, no predictions he can't back.

### Document the journey: the move itself as a series
**Evidence:** TikTok's "Moving to Dubai" and "Living in Dubai" discovery hubs show relocation-documentation is its own high-engagement genre — monthly cost-of-living breakdowns (rent, taxis, gym, insurance) and honest pros-and-cons videos consistently resonate, especially British-expat versions; day-in-the-life Dubai agent videos (including a first-day-on-the-job series) are an established viral sub-format on the platform per TikTok's own topic pages.
**Mechanics:** Serialise a real, stakes-attached life change in short episodes. The audience follows the person, not the niche, and the open loop (will it work?) compounds retention across videos. Real numbers and setbacks outperform highlight reels.
**Hook shapes:** I sold my car today. In 17 days I leave the UK for good. · We're moving to Dubai with £16,000 and a one-way ticket. Day 1. · Month one in Dubai as a brand-new broker: here's exactly what it cost.
**Samuel's version:** His strongest immediate format because it needs no audience or licence yet. Opening line: "I'm a Michelin-trained chef with a £250k property business, and in two and a half weeks I'm leaving it all to become a broker in Dubai. This is day one." Wife films (never appears), episodes cover the car sale, the flat, the £16k runway, first viewing day, first commission — with running totals on screen. Gym and faith appear as texture, not topics.

### Buyer-education series: "mistakes people make buying in Dubai"
**Evidence:** Reel-E's format analysis rates "things I wish I knew" hooks medium-high engagement and finds educational buyer-mistake content is the most-saved real-estate category (saves are the strongest distribution signal); Luxury Presence's hook research (109 tested hooks) and shhots.ai's 2026 format roundup both put mistake/warning explainers among the formats that reliably convert viewers into leads, and note they work best as a numbered series that gives people a reason to follow.
**Mechanics:** Name a specific costly mistake, explain it in plain terms with a real example, give one action. Warning framing beats tip framing because it triggers loss aversion, and series numbering builds follows.
**Hook shapes:** Three fees Dubai agents don't mention until you're signing. · This one clause cost a buyer 80,000 dirhams. Check for it. · Buying off-plan in Dubai? Watch this before you pay a deposit.
**Samuel's version:** "Before you buy in Dubai" — a numbered series filmed at the desk studio, occasionally in the kitchen while he cooks (the Michelin-level cooking is the scroll-stopper; the property lesson runs over it). Opening line: "I'm plating a dish that took me ten years to learn. Here's a Dubai buying mistake that takes ten seconds to avoid." Each episode: one mistake, one real sourced number, one thing to do about it. This is the format that most directly builds his target audience of Dubai buyers.

## BUSINESS

### Numbers-first talking head (Hormozi proof-promise-plan)
**Evidence:** Alex Hormozi's 2026 approach, broken down by Postiz, opens every short with 'proof, promise, plan' in three seconds, then a list or steps — fewer vlog cuts, more structure. Reap.video's breakdown of his Shorts confirms the pattern: bold statement or surprising stat first, direct-to-camera, hard captions. A study of 118,584+ viral shorts (virvid.ai) found Hook→Problem→Solution→CTA the dominant winning structure, and 'Results First' is one of the 9 highest-retention hook patterns in 2026 testing (socialync). Meta now uses a 1.0-second early-retention signal, so leading with the number is not stylistic, it is algorithmic (ugccopilot).
**Mechanics:** You state a real result of yours in the first sentence (the proof), promise what the viewer will learn from it, then deliver it as 3-5 fast, concrete steps to camera with big captions. The number does the hooking so the delivery can be plain.
**Hook shapes:** My company did £250,000 last year at a 75% margin. Here's the actual model. · I charge councils, not tourists. That one decision is why my margins are triple the industry average. · Most serviced accommodation operators make 20% margins. I make 75%. Three reasons.
**Samuel's version:** Desk studio, straight to camera: 'My property company turned over £250,000 last year with about a 75% margin, and I never dealt with a single tourist. Here's how it works.' Then 4 fast-cut steps: who the client is (UK councils), why councils pay more reliably than Airbnb guests, the cost structure that gets the margin to 75%, and the one risk nobody mentions. Real invoice numbers on screen as captions. Ends: 'Next video I'll show you what a month of this actually looks like.'

### Day-in-the-life with real stakes (documentary vlog)
**Evidence:** #dayinmylife and #dailyvlog sit at 35 billion combined views on TikTok and YouTube (PUSH.fm), and CORQ's TikTok business analysis found day-in-the-life vlogs are THE winning campaign format for macro creators because they produce the longest watch times — the strongest algorithm signal. The Social Cat and PlayPlay analyses both credit it to authenticity: unpolished moments outperform produced ones. Ashton Hall's morning-routine version pulled 1 billion views in 30 days (Man of Many, CNN) — proof discipline-lifestyle content has enormous ceiling, though half that audience came to mock the theatrics, which is exactly why an understated, believable version is the safer play.
**Mechanics:** Fast-cut footage of a genuinely eventful day (not a fake-perfect routine) with voiceover narration. Watch time comes from wanting to see how the day ends; trust comes from the boring, real bits left in.
**Hook shapes:** I sold my car yesterday. In 17 days I'm moving my family to Dubai. This is what today looked like. · Day in the life of someone dismantling a £250k business to start again at zero. · 5am, gym, then I spent the morning handing back the keys to my flat.
**Samuel's version:** Wife films (never appears): 'Twelve days until we move to Dubai. Sold the car Tuesday — got £9,200 for it. Today I'm handing the flat keys back.' Cuts: gym at 5am (5 seconds, no drama), packing boxes, a real phone call with the council about handover, cooking dinner properly in a half-empty kitchen, praying or a brief quiet moment (light touch). Ends on the runway number: '£16k in the account. That's what we're landing with. Following along? Tomorrow's the storage unit.'

### Open-books income and cost breakdown
**Evidence:** Income-report content is an established high-trust category — creators like Ty Myers built audiences on monthly 'how much I made' breakdowns (uscreen), and Influencers-Time's study of the cost-breakdown format found it works precisely because every number said aloud is traceable to a real document, making it the strongest mid-funnel trust format for premium offers. Failory and Bubble's build-in-public research both cite Pieter Levels publishing exact live revenue as the reason Nomad List became the default in its category: 'radical transparency creates trust' and personal accounts sharing real dashboards reliably outperform polished brand accounts.
**Mechanics:** You show the actual money — revenue in, costs out, what's left — on screen (bank app, spreadsheet, invoices) while narrating it plainly. Viewers stay because nobody shows real numbers, and the specificity is what converts followers into buyers who trust you with their own money.
**Hook shapes:** This is exactly what one council contract pays me per month. Screen's right there. · £250k in, and here's every cost that came out. The real margin, not the Instagram one. · Everyone shows you the Rolex. Nobody shows you the spreadsheet. Here's mine.
**Samuel's version:** Desk studio, laptop screen visible: 'One of my council contracts. £4,100 a month, this is the actual remittance.' Scroll the real document (sensitive bits blurred). 'Costs on this property: rent £1,450, bills £280, cleaning £190. That leaves about £2,100 a month from one house.' Plain delivery, no music swell. 'When I start brokering in Dubai I'll do the same thing — every deal, real numbers. If someone selling you property won't show you their numbers, ask why.'

### 'What £X buys you' price-anchored property tour
**Evidence:** PropCRM's 2026 Dubai agent guide names 'What AED X buys you in [community]' the most reliably viral real-estate format, and reports a single viral reel can generate 50+ qualified leads; Campaign Middle East documents reels literally driving Dubai's property boom. The winning mechanics per the Dubai marketing analyses: open on the most dramatic visual with a price text-overlay ('AED 2.5M gets you THIS in Dubai Marina'), guess-the-price hooks, and silent walkthroughs with text — viewers self-select by budget, which is why the leads are qualified, not just views.
**Mechanics:** Price in the first second, then a fast tour that either over- or under-delivers on what the viewer expected for that money. Comments fill with 'that's cheaper/dearer than my city', which drives reach; DMs fill with actual buyers.
**Hook shapes:** £300,000 in Manchester versus £300,000 in Dubai. Same money, watch this. · This apartment costs less than a terraced house in Bristol. It's on the 40th floor. · Guess what this costs. Everyone gets it wrong by at least £200k.
**Samuel's version:** First Dubai weeks: 'This is what £350,000 buys you in JVC.' Walk it fast — kitchen, view, pool — with the service charge, expected rent, and net yield as captions, sourced from the actual listing. Close plain: 'Same money in Leeds gets you a three-bed semi returning about 5%. This returns just over 7%, no income tax. I moved here two weeks ago to do this full time — if you want the real numbers on an area, comment the budget.' Series repeats at £200k, £500k, £1m.

### Cook-while-you-talk (story over Michelin-level cooking)
**Evidence:** 'Guy who tells stories while cooking' is a searched-for genre on TikTok in its own right (multiple TikTok discover pages), #TikTokFood carries 4.6M+ posts, and SideChef/Foodies Only analyses both find the top-performing cooking videos are the ones where the food is the retention device and the story is the content — Eitan Bernath's blend of narrative plus quick cuts being the cited model. The mechanics: hands-working footage is inherently watchable (the ASMR/process effect holds retention), while the voiceover carries a story that has nothing to do with the recipe. Almost nobody in business content can execute this at a genuinely elite culinary level, which makes it a low-competition lane.
**Mechanics:** Camera on the hands and pan at real Michelin technique level; voiceover tells one business or life story from start to finish, timed so the story pays off as the dish plates. Two retention engines running at once.
**Hook shapes:** I trained in Michelin kitchens for years. Now I sell property. Let me tell you why while I cook. · The week I nearly lost my biggest council contract. I'll tell you what happened while I make this. · Chefs make terrible employees and brilliant business owners. Here's why.
**Samuel's version:** Kitchen, overhead on the pan: 'People ask why a Michelin-trained chef ended up housing people for councils. I'll tell you while I cook this properly.' He butchers and pans a piece of fish at obvious pro level while telling one story plainly — the 90-hour weeks, the pay, the day he did the maths on what a head chef earns versus one rented house. Story lands as the plate is finished: 'That plate would sell for £38. The house I told you about makes £2,100 a month while I sleep. That's the whole reason. New story next week.'

### Build-in-public countdown series (episodic, numbered)
**Evidence:** Failory, Bubble and dev.to's 2026 build-in-public research converge on the same findings: people follow the person, not the company; founders who share struggles alongside wins and real metrics build the most loyal audiences; and 'the mundane reality of doing the thing every day, in public, is the most magnetic content a founder can make.' The socialync 2026 retention testing ranks Open Loop among the highest-scoring hook patterns — a numbered countdown ('Day 14 of...') is a built-in open loop, forcing follows because the story is unfinished. Samuel has what most build-in-public founders fake: a hard deadline, real money at risk, and a visible before/after.
**Mechanics:** Every video is an episode in one numbered, deadline-driven story ('X days until Dubai', then 'Month 1 as a broker'). Each episode gives one real update — a number, a setback, a decision — and ends on what happens next, so following the account is the only way to get the ending.
**Hook shapes:** 17 days until we leave for Dubai. £16,000 runway. I'm documenting every single day. · Day 4 in Dubai. I haven't earned a pound yet. Here's what I actually did today. · Month one as a Dubai broker: here's exactly what I made. It's not what you think.
**Samuel's version:** Pinned series, one per day: 'Sixteen days to go. Today the flat officially went. Here's where the money stands: car sold for £9,200, deposit back next week, £16k total landing money. The UK business keeps running — that's the safety net, £250k turnover, managed remotely. What I don't have is a single Dubai deal. Day one of fixing that is October 6th.' After landing it becomes 'Broker, week 3: viewings done, offers made, commission earned — real numbers every Friday.' Wife films throughout, never on camera.

## BOOKS

### The 30-Day Test (documentary experiment on a book principle)
**Evidence:** Matt D'Avella (4.03M subs, ~330M total views) built his channel on the '30 Day Experiments' playlist — self-imposed tests of habit/book principles filmed as daily vlogs cut into a cinematic narrative (youtube.com/playlist PLXKuahfdkl6z..., profilelayers.com/matt-davella). A direct book version exists and ranks: 'I tested the rules of Atomic Habits for 30 days. This is what happened' (youtube.com/watch?v=F6CfoUCtgV0), plus a wave of 'I Tried Atomic Habits' copycats on Medium/YouTube showing the format's pull. Grokipedia's book-summary-videos page notes creators use 15-60s Shorts of a single takeaway as the entry point to these longer tests — the short and the long video feed each other.
**Mechanics:** You publicly commit to living one book principle for a fixed period and film what actually happens, day-stamped, with a measurable result at the end. The stakes are real (the viewer wants to know if it worked), so each clip is a chapter in an open loop rather than advice.
**Hook shapes:** I tested the rules of Atomic Habits for 30 days. This is what happened. · Day 1 of doing everything this book says until I land in Dubai. · I gave this book 30 days to prove itself. Here are my numbers.
**Samuel's version:** Series: 'I'm running my Dubai move on one book.' He picks Atomic Habits (or 7 Habits' 'first things first') and films the actual 2.5-week countdown as the test — sold car, flat emptying, £16k runway on screen as a running counter, wife filming. Opening line, to camera at the desk: 'In 18 days I leave the UK for Dubai with £16,000. I'm going to run the whole move on the rules in this book, and I'll show you the numbers every day. Day one: I sold my car this morning.' Each episode = one rule, one real task, one real number. Final video: what the book got right and wrong, with the bank balance.

### The Credibility Listicle ('I've read X books — these N actually did it')
**Evidence:** Codie Sanchez: 'I Read 200 Books on Money: These 19 Will Make You Rich' — flagship enough to be dissected in newsletters (brainwash.substack.com/p/codie-sanchez-i-read-200-books-on). Multiple large finance channels run the identical template: 'I've Read 50 Books on Money… These 5 Will Make You Rich' (youtube.com/watch?v=GuQTSnf1EaM) and 'I've Read 40 Books… These 5 Will Make You Rich' (youtube.com/watch?v=LtMVToMHmSo), the latter carrying a Zapier sponsorship — advertisers only buy proven reach. The ftspod.com hook roundup confirms number-specific book hooks ('3 reasons…', 'the one sentence that…') are the top-performing author hooks, a format James Clear himself used constantly for Atomic Habits.
**Mechanics:** A big consumption number ('I read 200 books') buys authority in one line, then a short ranked list delivers only the survivors, each tied to a result the creator actually got. The gap between books-read and books-recommended is the hook.
**Hook shapes:** I read 200 books on money. These 19 will make you rich. · I've read 50 books on money. Only 5 changed what I actually do. · 3 books built this business. The other 40 were a waste of time.
**Samuel's version:** 'I'm a chef. Nobody taught me business. Three books took me from a Michelin kitchen to a £250,000-a-year property company — these are the three.' Filmed at the desk with his actual battered copies (Rich Dad, Psychology of Money, 7 Habits). For each book: the one thing he did because of it, with the real number it produced — e.g. 'Rich Dad is why I stopped chasing wages and rented property to councils instead. That contract does about 75% margin.' Ends: 'I'm reading the fourth one now, for Dubai. I'll tell you if it earns its place.'

### One Principle, One Video (Hormozi-caption talking head with real numbers)
**Evidence:** The Hormozi editing system (word-by-word ALL-CAPS captions, yellow keyword highlights, fast jump cuts, minimal b-roll) is documented to measurably lift completion rate — the dominant TikTok ranking signal at ~40-50% of algorithm weight (ascynd.io/en/blog/why-hormozi-captions-get-more-views, joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis). Mark Tilbury (~20M combined followers, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Tilbury) runs exactly this in finance: one money concept per short, delivered plainly with a concrete cost/number, e.g. his credit-card short at ~18M views. 60-second single-idea book summaries are a whole account category on TikTok (@60secbooksummarie's Atomic Habits summary).
**Mechanics:** One book idea per video, stated in the first line, then proven with the creator's own concrete numbers in 30-60 seconds. Karaoke-style captions and jump cuts hold the mute-scroll viewer to the end, and completion rate does the distribution.
**Hook shapes:** The Psychology of Money says keep room for error. Mine is £16,000. Here's why. · Rich Dad says buy assets, not liabilities. I sold my car last week. Here's the maths. · One page of this book is worth more than my culinary degree.
**Samuel's version:** Desk-studio shorts, one principle each, always cashed out in his own figures. Example — 'room for error' from Psychology of Money. Opening line: 'This book says always keep room for error. My room for error is £16,000. That's everything I'm taking to Dubai, and here's exactly how I got to that number.' Then 40 seconds: monthly costs in Dubai, months of runway, what happens at zero, what the book says he'd do wrong without it. Word-by-word captions, cut every 2-3 seconds, no b-roll needed beyond the bank app and the book.

### Show The Principle With Your Hands (visual demonstration, not explanation)
**Evidence:** FightMediocrity (1M+ subs) proved book ideas travel furthest as visuals — hand-drawn summaries plus the creator's own application (medium.com/@pascal.gerardus book-summary channel guide, africanhotspot.com roundup). Mark Tilbury's biggest hits are propped demonstrations, not lectures: visualising billionaire net worths, breaking down a jar of Nutella's real cost, and the ~18M-view credit-card sketch (creatordb.app/creatorstats/mark-tilbury). The pattern across both: an abstract book concept made physical outperforms the same concept spoken.
**Mechanics:** Take one book concept and demonstrate it with physical objects and a skill the viewer can watch, so the idea lands visually before a word is needed. The demonstration is also the retention device — people watch skilled hands to the end.
**Hook shapes:** This is what a jar of Nutella actually costs you. (Tilbury-style prop breakdown) · Atomic Habits in one drawing. (FightMediocrity-style) · Rich Dad's most famous lesson, on a chopping board.
**Samuel's version:** His unfair advantage: Michelin-level knife work on camera. Example — the 1% rule from Atomic Habits, filmed in the kitchen. Opening line, already cutting: 'I trained in a Michelin kitchen. This book's 1% rule is just how a kitchen works, and I can show you in 60 seconds.' He shows the same cut done badly then perfectly — 'nobody gets this in a day, you get it doing 200 of these every service for two years' — then the turn: 'I applied the same thing to property. Year one I made almost nothing. Year three the business did £250,000.' Rich Dad version: assets vs liabilities laid out as ingredients on the board — what feeds you tomorrow vs what you consume tonight — plates instead of diagrams.

### The One Sentence (held book, pointed finger, then the story it caused)
**Evidence:** The holding-the-book, on-screen-passage format is the core BookTok unit — #booktok sits in the tens of billions of views, with self-help titles like Untamed riding it (huffingtonpost.co.uk booktok self-help piece, publishdrive.com BookTok author guide). ftspod.com's author-hook data ranks 'The one sentence that changed…' among the best-converting book hooks. The transformation frame 'the books that took me from X to Y' is a documented viral formula (postiz.com hook framework), and micro-stories are shown to trigger action in under 10 seconds when they open in the action, not the setup (buffer.com/resources/storytelling-formulas, trivision.com short-form storytelling).
**Mechanics:** Open on the physical book and one highlighted sentence, claim it caused a specific real decision, then spend the video telling that decision as a fast story with receipts. The sentence is the hook; the creator's life is the proof.
**Hook shapes:** This sentence is the reason I'm leaving the UK. · The one sentence that changed how I think about money. · I read this line three years ago. Last week I finally did it.
**Samuel's version:** Who Moved My Cheese, camera on the highlighted line about what happens when you don't change. Opening: holds the book to lens, points — 'This sentence is why I'm leaving England in two weeks.' Then the story, fast cuts over real footage of the flat being packed: 'I've got a business here doing £250,000 a year renting accommodation to councils. Margins around 75%. Everyone says you don't walk away from that. But the whole business depends on council budgets I don't control — that's my cheese, and it can move. So we sold the car, we're giving up the flat, and I'm going to Dubai with £16,000 to learn property from the buying side. I'll show you every step.' Closes on the book going into the moving box.

## LINKEDIN

### The Milestone Announcement (text-first post + 30-60s clip of the real moment)
**Evidence:** Milestone/decision posts are LinkedIn's most reliable engagement format: personal stories consistently outperform polished advice (Postiv/Postbeam 2026 analyses), and Autoposting.ai's study of 1,200+ founder accounts found founders documenting real decisions (selling assets, pivots, moves) get the platform's highest engagement rates. Van der Blom's 2025 report (1.8M posts) shows native video earned a 69% performance boost, especially vertical <60s with captions — and 80%+ of LinkedIn video is watched muted, so the moment must read silent.
**Mechanics:** A plain text post announces an irreversible life/business decision, with a short native video of the actual moment attached as proof. On LinkedIn the TEXT is the hook (first 200 characters before 'see more' decide everything) and the clip is evidence — the opposite of Instagram, where the first second of footage is the hook and the caption barely matters.
**Hook shapes:** I sold my car this morning. · In 17 days my wife and I leave the UK for good. · Yesterday I handed back the keys to the flat we got married from.
**Samuel's version:** Post text: 'I sold my car this morning. In 18 days we leave the UK for Dubai. I run a business here that makes £250k a year. People keep asking me why I'd walk away from that. Here's the honest answer.' Attached: 40-second captioned clip — keys handed over, empty parking space, Samuel to camera at the desk: 'That car was the last big thing keeping us here.' End the post with the one real reason (family, the ceiling he hit in the UK), not a moral.

### Build-in-Public Numbers Post (real figures, weekly cadence, text + video)
**Evidence:** Autoposting.ai's 1,200-founder analysis: founders sharing actual revenue numbers, costs and runway are the single highest-engaging account type, with qualified inbound starting months 2-3. The 2025-2026 algorithm shift (LinkedIn's 360Brew ranking, per Dataslayer/van der Blom) rewards dwell time and 'depth and authority' — specific numbers hold readers 30+ seconds, which now outranks 50 quick likes. Text posts with real figures also drive more comments and business enquiries than video (Lilach Bullock's video-vs-text test).
**Mechanics:** A recurring transparent ledger of the move and the business: exact runway, exact costs, exact revenue, what changed this week. Numbers in the text, a 30-60s captioned clip showing the reality behind one number. Same clip on Instagram is a vlog moment; on LinkedIn the numbers in the text are the content and the clip is texture.
**Hook shapes:** £16,000. That's the entire runway for moving my family to Dubai. · My UK business did £250k last year at roughly 75% margin. Here's why that number stopped being enough. · Week 1 in Dubai. Here's exactly what it cost.
**Samuel's version:** Weekly series: '£16k runway. Week 2. Flights booked: £1,840. First month's rent in Dubai: I'll know Thursday. The business back home paid out on time — that's the safety net, not the plan.' Clip: Samuel at the desk with the actual spreadsheet on screen, fast cuts to the packing, one line to camera: 'I'm showing the real numbers because every Dubai video you've seen hides them.' This is also the trust engine for future buyers — a broker who was transparent about his own money.
